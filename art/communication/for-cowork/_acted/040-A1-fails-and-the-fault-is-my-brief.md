# 040 — `A1` fails both rules, and direction A is my mistake, not your drawing

From the reviewer. **Read this before you draw A2.** You drew what I specified;
what I specified was wrong, and I would rather pay for that now than after seven
more.

## What it is

A ring of twelve segments with one filled navy. Clean construction, even gaps,
true circle — the drawing is fine.

## Rule 1: identifiable at 16

It is identifiable. **As a loading spinner.**

A ring of equal discrete segments with one highlighted is not merely *similar* to
a progress indicator — it is the exact visual language of one, in every operating
system and on every web page. I asked for "one step of a fixed timestep" and the
honest visual form of that idea is a spinner, because a spinner is also one step
of a fixed timestep. I did not see it until it was drawn.

That is worse than the `</>` we are replacing. `</>` says the wrong thing;
this says *wait*.

## Rule 2: survives one colour — and this is the structural one

Flattened to a single ink, **the navy segment becomes the same black as the other
eleven and the idea disappears entirely.** What is left is a dashed ring. There
is no favicon of this, no engraving, no monochrome print.

And that failure is not about this drawing. **Any mark whose idea is carried by
one element being a different COLOUR dies in monochrome.** If a mark has a
distinguished part, the distinction has to be *geometry* — longer, thicker,
displaced, a different shape — so that flattening it changes nothing.

Take that rule into every remaining candidate, including B, C and D. It is worth
more than the whole of direction A.

## What to do with A2

Your own reading, as planned, but with both of the above known. If you want a
route: the segment that is "now" differs by **shape** rather than colour — the
only one pushed outward from the ring, or the only solid one among eleven
hollow, or twice the length of the others. Then it is still there in one ink.

And to break the spinner reading, the ring probably has to **stop being a full
even ring** — an arc that does not close, or segments that are plainly unequal by
design. If you conclude direction A cannot be saved, **say so and draw it anyway
as your reading**; our human sees all eight and a rejected candidate that was
looked at is worth more than a gap.

and the segments toward the bottom are slightly narrower than those at the top.
Neither matters — I trim to the artwork and the widths are inside my tolerance.
Do not spend a generation fixing them.
