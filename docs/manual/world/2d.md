# 2D games

A 2D game in LuauG is made of the same world as a 3D one: instances under
`Workspace`, the same scripts, scenes, undo and physics tick. What makes it 2D
is three things: sprites and tiles lie on the world's **XY plane**, the camera
looks at that plane through an **orthographic** lens, and the plane has
**physics of its own**. A game may mix the two -- a 2D level in front of a 3D
background, or 3D props on a tilemap.

`examples/20-platformer` is all of it in one file: a painted level, a hero that
runs and jumps, coins and a flag.

## The camera

A `Camera` with `Projection` set to `Orthographic` shows `OrthographicSize`
metres above and below the middle of the view, at every distance. Its width
follows the window's shape. Put it anywhere in front of the plane, looking down
-Z -- which is where a fresh `CFrame` looks:

```luau
--!strict
local camera = Instance.new("Camera")
camera.Projection = Enum.CameraProjection.Orthographic
camera.OrthographicSize = 6
camera.CFrame = CFrame.new(0, 4, 50)
camera.Parent = workspace
workspace.CurrentCamera = camera
```

Zooming is changing `OrthographicSize`. Following a character is moving the
camera's position in x and y, as the platformer does.

## Sprites: `Part2D`

A `Part2D` is a picture and a body in one, as a `Part` is a mesh and a body.

```luau
--!strict
local crate = Instance.new("Part2D")
crate.Size = Vector2.new(1, 1)
crate.Position = Vector2.new(3, 5)
crate.Image = "asset://sprites/crate.png" :: Content
crate.Filter = Enum.TextureFilter.Nearest -- pixel art stays crisp
crate.Parent = workspace
```

- **`Position` is its middle and `Rotation` is in degrees**, counter-clockwise.
- **`Shape`** -- `Box`, `Circle` or `Capsule` -- is both what it collides as and
  what it is drawn as: a circle cuts its picture round.
- **`ImageRectOffset` and `ImageRectSize`** pick one frame of a sprite sheet, in
  pixels. `FlipX` faces a character the other way.
- **`ZIndex`** decides which of two overlapping sprites is in front. At one
  `ZIndex`, a tilemap is beneath a part, and otherwise the one made later is in
  front.
- Sprites are **unlit**: a picture is drawn as it was painted, times `Color`.

### Animated sprites: `SpriteAnimator`

A `SpriteAnimator` under a `Part2D` plays frames of its sheet on the simulation
clock, so a walk cycle is data and not a script:

```luau
--!strict
local hero = Instance.new("Part2D")
hero.Image = "asset://sprites/hero.png" :: Content
hero.Parent = workspace

local walk = Instance.new("SpriteAnimator")
walk.FrameSize = Vector2.new(16, 16) -- pixels
walk.Columns = 8 -- frames to a row of the sheet
walk.SheetOffset = Vector2.new(0, 32) -- the walk is the third row
walk.FrameCount = 6
walk.FramesPerSecond = 10
walk.Parent = hero
walk.Playing = true
```

- Each tick it plays, it writes the sprite's `ImageRectOffset` and
  `ImageRectSize`, so what it does replicates, undoes and fires `Changed` like
  any other write.
- Frames count along the sheet's rows from `SheetOffset`, starting at
  `FirstFrame`. `Frame` says which one it is on.
- With `Looped` off, it stops on the last frame and sets `Playing` to false:
  `GetPropertyChangedSignal("Playing")` is its end event. Played again, it
  starts from the top; paused and resumed, it carries on.
- One sheet holds several animations: give each its own animator, or change
  `SheetOffset` and `FrameCount` when the character starts running.

## Physics on the plane

A `Part2D` that is not `Anchored` falls, bounces and is pushed. Its body is
simulated by a 2D physics library on the same fixed tick as the 3D world, so a
game runs the same on every machine.

- `Velocity`, `AngularVelocity` and `ApplyImpulse` move it, and the simulation
  writes the first two back every tick.
- `FixedRotation` keeps a character upright; `GravityScale` makes one fall
  faster or float. The plane's gravity is `Workspace.Gravity`'s x and y.
- **`Sensor`** makes it report what enters it and push nothing: a coin, a
  checkpoint, a trigger.
- **`Touched` and `TouchEnded`** fire when something begins and stops touching
  it -- another `Part2D`, or a tilemap's solid tiles.
- `CollisionGroup` uses the same groups as 3D parts.

`Workspace:Raycast2D` asks what a line on the plane hits first:

```luau
--!strict
local hero = Instance.new("Part2D")
hero.Parent = workspace
local ignoreHero = RaycastParams.new({ Filter = { hero :: Instance } })
local below = workspace:Raycast2D(hero.Position, Vector2.new(0, -1), ignoreHero)
if below ~= nil then
    print(below.Instance.Name, below.Distance)
end
```

### Joints

A joint holds two `Part2D`s together. It names the two parts, `Part0` and
`Part1`, and a point on each, `Anchor0` and `Anchor1`, in that part's own metres
from its middle, so the point turns with the part.

```luau
--!strict
local frame = Instance.new("Part2D")
frame.Anchored = true
frame.Parent = workspace
local door = Instance.new("Part2D")
door.Size = Vector2.new(0.2, 2)
door.Position = Vector2.new(0, -1)
door.Parent = workspace

local hinge = Instance.new("HingeConstraint2D")
hinge.Part0 = frame
hinge.Part1 = door
hinge.Anchor1 = Vector2.new(0, 1) -- the door's top edge
hinge.LimitsEnabled = true
hinge.LowerAngle = -90
hinge.UpperAngle = 90
hinge.Parent = workspace
```

- **`HingeConstraint2D`** is a point both parts turn about: a door, a wheel, a
  pendulum. Its limits are degrees from how the two stood when it was made, and
  `MotorEnabled` drives it towards `MotorSpeed`, in degrees per second, with at
  most `MotorMaxTorque`.
- **`SpringConstraint2D`** keeps a distance, softly: `Length` at rest,
  `Stiffness` in hertz, `Damping` from 0 (it bounces for ever) to 1 (it settles
  without overshooting), and never shorter than `MinLength` or longer than
  `MaxLength`. **A `Stiffness` of 0 is a rigid rod**, which is what a rope
  bridge's links are.
- **`WeldConstraint2D`** holds the two rigidly, as they stood when it was made.
- `CollideConnected` is off by default, so two parts that overlap at a joint by
  construction do not push each other apart. `Enabled` switches a joint off
  without removing it.
- A joint that cannot hold -- a part missing, or out of the world, or two
  anchored parts -- holds nothing, and is not an error.

## Tiles: `Tilemap2D`

A `Tilemap2D` is a grid of tiles cut from one image, the **tileset**. Tile 0 is
empty; tile `n` is the `n`-th tile of the tileset, counting along its rows from
the top left.

```luau
--!strict
local level = Instance.new("Tilemap2D")
level.Tileset = "asset://sprites/tiles.png" :: Content
level.TileSize = Vector2.new(16, 16) -- pixels, in the tileset
level.CellSize = 1 -- metres, in the world
level:FillRect(0, 0, 39, 1, 2) -- a floor, two cells deep
level:SetCell(12, 2, 3)
level.Parent = workspace
```

- **Every non-empty tile is solid** while `Collides` is on. A tilemap collides
  as the outline of its solid tiles rather than as a box per tile, so a
  character runs across a floor of tiles without catching on the seams.
- A background layer is a second tilemap with `Collides` off and a lower
  `ZIndex`.
- Cells are whole numbers and y grows upwards, as the world's does. A map is
  stored in blocks of 16 by 16 cells, so it is as large as what is painted.

## In multiplayer

A replicated game's 2D world looks the same on every machine:

- **Another player's sprites are drawn between snapshots**, a few ticks in the
  past, as their 3D parts are, so they glide rather than step. A sprite turning
  through 180 degrees takes the short way round.
- **A tilemap is replicated.** A replica is sent every block of it when it
  arrives, then each block an edit on the authority changed, so a wall broken or
  a floor built during play is broken or built everywhere. A replica's own edit
  to it is overwritten by the next block the authority sends.
- Joints and animators stay on the authority. What they do -- where the parts
  are, and which frame a sprite shows -- is what travels.

## In the editor

The **Tiles** panel paints the selected tilemap -- or the first in the world --
from a palette of its tileset: **Paint** lays the chosen tile along the pointer's
path, **Erase** empties it, and each stroke is one undo step. It acts only while
its panel is on screen.

Its **2D view** checkbox turns the viewport square to the plane: the right mouse
button pans, the wheel zooms about the pointer, and turning it off gives the 3D
view back where it was. Sprites are selected by clicking them -- the one drawn on
top -- and moved, turned and sized with the usual manipulator.
