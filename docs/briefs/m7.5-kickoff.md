# M7.5 Kickoff — Looking Like an Engine: Shadows, Lights, Reflections

- Started: 2026-08-21
- Roadmap section: docs/roadmap.md#m75--looking-like-an-engine-shadows-lights-reflections-l

## Goal (restated)

A person who has used Unity or Unreal should not be able to tell, from a still
frame, which engine drew it. That sentence is the whole milestone, and ADR 0038
is what turns it from a feeling into a list: M4 shipped one shadow cascade at
5.9 cm per texel, eight unculled lights per draw, no image-based lighting at all,
and no post chain beyond the tonemap resolve. Each of those four is a named
technique with published parameters, and each is replaced here.

The one thing that is not about looking: two thousand objects sharing one mesh
cost two thousand draw calls today, which is 6.9 ms of CPU submission in an
11.1 ms frame. The renderer's second half is also where that stops.

## Scope checklist (from roadmap)

- [x] **Cascaded shadow maps** — four cascades, practical split scheme
      (GPU Gems 3 ch. 10) with a `λ` blend of uniform and logarithmic
      partitioning; **normal-offset bias** replacing depth-only; a hardware
      comparison sampler so a tap degrades instead of switching; PCF between
      2×2 and 7×7.
  - [x] The PCF radius is constant in **world** space across cascades.
  - [x] Cascades **blend over a band**, not switch at a plane.
- [x] **Clustered forward shading** — Olsson and Assarsson, 16×9×24 grid,
      exponential depth slicing `slice = max(log2(linearDepth) · scale + bias, 0)`.
- [x] **Image-based lighting** — split-sum (Karis 2013): a prefiltered
      environment whose mip chain is indexed by roughness, plus a 2D BRDF LUT
      indexed by (N·V, roughness).
- [x] **The post chain**: exposure, bloom, an ambient occlusion term,
      anti-aliasing.
- [x] **Instanced draws for repeated meshes** — group the run `extract` already
      sorts into one call with a per-instance transform buffer.
- [x] Design constraint: **the scene's depth must be samplable by a later pass**,
      decided while the pass list is being rebuilt rather than afterwards.
- [x] Design constraint: **motion vectors and a jitterable projection are
      renderer outputs**, not private state of an anti-aliasing pass; the ui2d
      pass stays separate from the world pass.
- [x] Deliverable: `examples/02-meshes` and the M7 streaming example, both
      through the new path, each beside its M4.5 render at the same camera and
      clock.

## NOT in scope

- Anything on R15's closed list — no editor, no multiplayer, no 2D layer, no
  mobile port, no navmesh.
- **Ray tracing**, in any form.
- **Virtualized geometry or virtualized shadow maps.**
- **Global illumination beyond what a prefiltered environment gives.** No
  probes-per-object, no light maps, no voxel or screen-space GI beyond the
  ambient occlusion term named above.
- **Temporal upscaling** — no FSR, no XeSS, no DLSS. The roadmap's own note says
  why the door is left open rather than walked through.
- **Temporal anti-aliasing.** The post chain owes anti-aliasing; it does not owe
  a velocity buffer with no consumer. Decision 10 says what that costs and what
  it does not foreclose.
- **Hand-placed reflection probes.** The roadmap leaves this as a judgement call
  for this brief and the call is: no. v1 has no editor to place them with
  (ADR 0017), and a probe API a script has to place by hand is an editor feature
  wearing a script's clothes. One environment, generated from the sky the engine
  already draws, is what a day/night cycle actually wants.
- **The graphics-settings family** — shadow resolution, cascade count, render
  scale, light budget, post toggles as engine settings. ADR 0038 §3 puts that at
  M8 on purpose: until this milestone exists there is nothing worth exposing.
- **Area lights, light shadows other than the sun's, volumetrics.** A spot light
  still casts no shadow; `RenderLight::shadows` remains the field nothing reads.
- **Compute shaders.** Absent from the RHI and absent from here; every technique
  below is chosen to need none.

## The decisions this brief makes

The RHI was frozen at the end of M4 (ADR 0037) and ADR 0038 predicted this
milestone would test it: *"Cubemaps, mip-chain generation, compute for
prefiltering, and array textures for cascades are the most likely places the
frozen 32 calls turn out to be 32 minus something."* Four of the decisions below
exist because that prediction was right and the answer, three times out of four,
was a different technique rather than a wider interface. **One field is added,
and ADR 0043 is what asks for it.**

### 1. Four cascades live in one 2×2 atlas, not a texture array

`TextureDesc` has a `layers` field and `ColorAttachment`/`DepthStencilAttachment`
have no way to name a layer — so a four-layer array can be created and cannot be
rendered into. An atlas needs nothing: one 2048² `D32Float` target, four 1024²
tiles, and `setViewport`/`setScissor` per cascade. **1024 per tile and not the
2048 M4 shipped for its single cascade**, and that is not a downgrade: four tiles
at 2048 would be a 4096 atlas at 64 MiB, and 4096 is exactly the 2D texture size
the weakest conforming device is required to support, which is not a floor to
build a default on (R16). The near cascade still lands at about 1.1 cm per texel
against M4's 5.9. One texture is also one bind
and one `GetDimensions`, which the sampling side wants anyway.

**What it costs, and it is a real cost rather than a footnote:** a PCF tap near a
tile edge reads the neighbouring cascade's depth, which is a bright seam along
the split. Every sample is therefore clamped to its own tile, inset by the kernel
radius. That clamp is the atlas's whole tax and it is four lines.

### 2. The comparison sampler is `Gather`, and `SamplerDesc` stays frozen

ADR 0038 asks for "a hardware comparison sampler so a tap degrades instead of
switching". The property that matters is the degradation — a tap that returns
0.25 instead of flipping 0 to 1 — and `SampleCmp`'s 0.25 is bilinear weights over
four binary comparisons. `Gather` returns those same four texels in one texture
operation, and the four comparisons and the two lerps are six ALU instructions.

So the result is `SampleCmp`'s, arithmetically, at one texture op per tap, and
the frozen `SamplerDesc` gains no `compareEnable`. **This is the freeze working:**
the interface was not widened because the technique did not need it, and the
thing ADR 0038 actually asked for is delivered.

### 3. The PCF radius is world-constant and the cascades blend

The roadmap names these as the two tells of a first CSM and it is right, so they
are requirements rather than polish. Each cascade has its own world-units-per-texel;
the kernel is therefore sized in metres and converted per cascade, so shadow
softness does not change when an object crosses a split. And the last twelfth of each
cascade's range blends into the next by sampling both and lerping — a hard
handover is visible for exactly the same reason.

### 4. Image-based lighting is octahedral, CPU-prefiltered, and its source is the sky

Three of the four things a normal IBL implementation uses are absent from the
frozen RHI: there is no cube texture type, no `generateMipmaps`, and no compute
to prefilter with. One thing is present, and it is the one that matters:
**`uploadTexture(texture, data, mipLevel)` takes a mip level.**

So the environment is a single 2D texture in **octahedral** projection with a
real mip chain, each level prefiltered on the CPU for one roughness and uploaded
to its own level. Octahedral rather than a cube because a cube needs a type the
RHI does not have; the seam cost is one wrap in the decode, and the quality at
128² per level is indistinguishable at the roughnesses a mip chain is indexed by.

**And the source is the analytic sky the engine already draws.** That is the
decision that makes the whole thing worth having rather than a feature waiting
for content: `sky.hlsl` computes a horizon, a zenith and a sun disc from
`Lighting`'s own state, so the same function on the CPU produces an environment
that changes when `ClockTime` does. A metal reflects the sky at the hour the
script set. No asset, no probe placement, no editor.

Diffuse irradiance is **9 spherical-harmonic coefficients** projected from the
same sky and carried in the frame uniforms, which is what replaces the flat
`Lighting.Ambient` on the diffuse lobe. `Ambient` does not go away — it is added
to the irradiance, which is what makes it still mean what its documentation says.

### 5. The prefilter is amortised, because a day/night cycle changes it every frame

Regenerating six mips of an environment every time `ClockTime` moves would put
the prefilter in the frame. So: the sky's parameters are hashed, a change beyond
a threshold marks the chain dirty, and **one mip is rebuilt per frame** —
finest first, so the reflection sharpens rather than appearing. The job pool M7
built does the texel loop. A scene whose sky never moves pays this once.

### 6. Clustered forward: the cluster data travels as textures

A fragment shader's only bulk-data route in the frozen RHI is a sampled texture —
there are no storage buffers, and a uniform block is capped at four per stage.
So three textures carry it: the cluster grid (`R32Float`, one texel per cluster,
packing offset and count), the light index list (`R32Float`), and the light
parameters themselves (`Rgba32Float`, three texels per light). All three are read
with `Load`, which needs no filtering and no mips; the sampler bound beside each
is a formality SDL_GPU's binding model requires.

The budget stops being eight and becomes **256 lights in the frame and 64 in any
one cluster** — and the number that matters is the second one, because that is
what a fragment pays for.

### 7. Light assignment is CPU-side and bounded by each light's own extent

3,456 clusters times every light is not affordable on a frame thread. Each light
touches a contiguous block: its depth extent gives a slice range through the same
exponential mapping the shader uses, and its screen-space bounding square gives
the x and y ranges. The exact sphere-versus-cluster-AABB test then runs only
inside that block. Ordering is by light index within a cluster and by cluster
index across the list, so the whole build is a pure function of the snapshot
(R10) even when the job pool splits it.

### 8. A depth prepass is what makes depth samplable — and it does not solve water

This is the roadmap's design constraint, answered while the pass list is open
rather than after it closed.

**What ships:** a depth-only prepass writes the scene's depth into the
`D32Float` texture; ambient occlusion reads that texture in its own pass; the
forward pass then attaches the same texture with `LoadOp::Load` and does not
write it. Depth is a texture and an attachment at different times, never at once,
which is a rule every backend enforces and none has to be asked about.

**What does not ship, stated because "we already have depth" is exactly the
half-truth the roadmap warns about:** the *blended* pass has depth attached, so
a water fragment there still cannot sample the opaque depth behind it. Foam needs
either a read-only depth attachment state or a texture copy, and the frozen RHI
has neither. It is one field or one call, it is an ADR, and the milestone that
ships water is the one that should write it.

The prepass has a second effect worth naming: it is a second geometry submission,
which is CPU cost in the exact place Decision 9 is spending effort to remove.
Instancing is what pays for it, and the perf table records both.

### 9. Instancing gets the one RHI field the struct was already shaped for

`VertexBufferLayout`'s own comment says it: *"Per-instance stepping arrives with
instanced rendering (M4); leaving it out keeps the capture stream from carrying a
field no backend reads."* The field is now read. **ADR 0043** adds
`VertexBufferLayout::perInstance`, defaulting to false, and that is the entire
change to the frozen surface.

The alternative was a vertex-stage texture fetch keyed on `SV_InstanceID`, which
needs nothing new. It was rejected because `uploadTexture` writes a whole mip: a
64K-instance texture would upload four megabytes every frame to draw two hundred
enemies, while `upload(buffer, data, offset)` writes exactly the prefix in use.
The workaround would have cost more than the field.

Grouping is `extract`'s, not the backend's — the roadmap's own third design
constraint. `extract` already sorts by pipeline, material and mesh, so an
instanced run is a run of equal sort keys, and what is added is the run detection
and the transform ring buffer.

**Transparent draws are never instanced.** Their order *is* their correctness.

### 10. Anti-aliasing is FXAA, and TAA's two prerequisites are named rather than built

The post chain owes anti-aliasing. FXAA is spatial, costs about 0.2 ms at 1080p,
and needs neither a velocity buffer nor a jittered projection — so it is what
ships.

The roadmap asks for something else in the same breath, and it is not the same
request: *declare* motion vectors and a jitterable projection as renderer outputs
so a later temporal pass is days rather than a milestone. What that costs, split
honestly:

- **The jitter ships**, because it is five lines and one field. `RenderCamera`
  carries a sub-pixel offset that `extract` folds into the projection; it is zero
  everywhere, so every golden is unchanged, and there is exactly one place a
  temporal pass would set it.
- **The velocity buffer does not ship**, because writing a second render target
  in the forward pass and carrying a previous transform on every `DrawItem` is
  renderer-wide bandwidth for a consumer that does not exist. That is the
  speculative abstraction the review bar forbids. What it would take is written
  down here instead: a second `ColorAttachment` on the forward passes, a
  `previousTransform` on `DrawItem`, and one more matrix in the instance stride.
- **The ui2d pass stays separate from the world pass.** M6 built it that way and
  the roadmap says this sentence is the whole of what this milestone owes frame
  generation. It is a thing not to break, and it is not broken.

### 11. Exposure is automatic, and `ExposureCompensation` is the artist's control

A day/night cycle is exactly the case a fixed exposure of 1.0 fails: noon blows
out and midnight is black, which is what makes the current image read as
"unfinished" more than any single missing effect. So the tonemap's exposure is
driven by the frame's own average log-luminance, adapted over time.

The chain is four tiny passes — HDR to 64², then 16², then 4², then a 1×1 that
also does the adaptation by reading last frame's 1×1 — which is the shape
render-to-mip would have collapsed into one. Ping-pong between two 1×1 targets
carries the state.

**Adaptation is driven by the simulation's fixed step and never by a wall clock**
(R10 in spirit, and goldens in practice): a screenshot at frame 30 has to be the
same picture on a fast machine and a slow one.

On top of it, `Lighting.ExposureCompensation` in EV stops, which is the API
addition this milestone makes and the one a person coming from Roblox already
knows the name of.

### 12. Bloom is a progressive chain of separate textures

Six downsample passes and five upsample passes, the dual-filter shape Call of
Duty published — thirteen-tap down, tent up, which is what keeps a bright pixel
from becoming a flickering box. Each level is its own texture rather than a mip,
for the same reason as everything else here: a `ColorAttachment` names a texture
and not a level.

The threshold is soft-kneed rather than hard, because a hard threshold makes
bloom pop as a surface crosses it, and it is a scene-referred threshold applied
after exposure so it means the same thing at noon and at dusk.

### 14. The sun has a colour, and the sky knows what hour it is

Not in the roadmap's list, and it belongs to this milestone anyway: the
environment IS the sky (Decision 4), so a sky that does not change cannot make a
reflection that does. Until now `GpuFrameUniforms` carried
`SunDirectionBrightness` and no colour at all, so the sun cast white light at
every hour while the sky's disc was tinted by a constant nobody set.

What is derived, from the sun's elevation and nothing else: its own colour, warm
within a few degrees of the horizon; the zenith, which darkens at night; a day
factor that multiplies the sun's brightness so a sun below the horizon lights
nothing rather than lighting every upward-facing surface from underneath; and
the disc's brightness relative to the sky, which is what puts a highlight in a
mirror rather than a pale smudge.

`Lighting.FogColor` keeps its documented meaning exactly -- at midday the tint is
white and the horizon band is the property unchanged.

### 15. The exposure key is a calibration and says so

Photography's middle grey is 0.18, and 0.18 is wrong here. It is middle grey in
PHOTOMETRIC units, and this engine's radiance unit is arbitrary:
`Lighting.Brightness` defaults to 2.6 because 2.6 looked right, not because it is
2.6 of anything. Borrowing the constant darkened every existing scene by about a
stop and a third.

So the key is 0.45, and the method is stated where the constant is: every scene
in the repository was authored against the fixed exposure of 1.0 this pass
replaced, so the key is chosen to leave them at the level they were authored at.
That makes automatic exposure a **stabiliser** -- it holds a scene's level as its
light changes -- rather than a change of level applied to all existing content.
The day the engine gains photometric lights, it becomes 0.18 and every scene's
brightness becomes a number in lux.

### 13. Ambient occlusion is screen-space, from the prepass depth, and it darkens ambient only

An SSAO term applied to direct light is wrong — a surface's occlusion of the
*environment* says nothing about whether the sun reaches it, and the sun already
has a shadow map that answers that exactly. So the AO texture multiplies the
image-based and ambient terms and nothing else, which is both the correct thing
and the one that survives a comparison against the reference.

Sixteen taps on a spiral, radius in world units projected to screen, with a
depth-aware 4×4 blur. Normals are reconstructed from depth rather than written to
a G-buffer, because a forward renderer that grows a normal target has grown half
a deferred one.

## Build order

IBL first — the roadmap's own note, and it is right: it is the largest visual
change for the least work and it depends on nothing else here. Then shadows,
then lights, then the passes that need a prepass, then the chain, then
instancing, which is the only item that is about the CPU.

1. ADR 0043 and the one RHI field, with `rhi_null` and `rhi_capture` updated so
   the freeze's own proof still holds.
2. Octahedral environment, SH irradiance, BRDF LUT, and the split-sum in
   `luaug_brdf.hlsli`. The differential: a metal sphere at `ClockTime` 6 and at
   12 must not render identically.
3. Cascades: the atlas, the practical split, normal-offset bias, `Gather` PCF,
   world-constant radius, the blend band.
4. Clusters: the CPU build, the three textures, the shader's loop over one
   cluster's list. The differential: light 9 must light something.
5. Depth prepass and SSAO.
6. Bloom, auto-exposure, FXAA.
7. Instancing, and the horde measurement that says whether it did anything.
8. The deliverable pair, the perf table, the re-recorded goldens, the Gate
   Record.

## Subagent plan

Orchestrator-only, and this milestone is the clearest case for it in the project
so far. Every item above touches the same three files — `renderer_default.cpp`,
`luaug_brdf.hlsli`, `shader_types.h` — and the pass list is one artifact with one
order. MASTER_PROMPT §7's list of things not to fan out reads like a description
of this work: cross-cutting refactors, anything touching two modules' seams,
scheduler and kernel work.

What can fan out, and will if it earns it: an adversarial review of the finished
diff against R3, R10, R17 and ADR 0038's stated parameters, and a research
verifier for any HLSL intrinsic whose behaviour under SDL_shadercross is a guess
rather than a fact.

## Attempted / abandoned

- **Middle grey at 0.18.** Photography's constant, borrowed for the exposure key,
  and it darkened every scene in the repository by about a stop and a third. It
  is middle grey in PHOTOMETRIC units and this engine has none; the key is a
  calibration and Decision 15 says so.
- **Amortising the environment prefilter one level per frame, conditionally.**
  Correct as an idea and wrong as an implementation: it made a frame's SHAPE
  depend on its history, which `clock_differential` refuses. The upload is
  unconditional now and only the baking is amortised.
- **Disabling image-based lighting to price it.** The per-feature sweep works by
  removing one pass and rebuilding, and removing the environment sample from the
  shader changes its declared resource counts, so the pipeline stops being
  creatable and the measurement reads zero draws. It is the one row the sweep
  could not produce, and the resolution comparison is what stood in for it.

## Findings

Twelve, and six of them are about how a defect was found rather than about what
it was. That ratio is not an accident: this milestone's output is pixels, and
pixels are the one thing a unit test cannot look at.

1. **The picture found what every unit test passed on.** Twelve lights on a floor
   produced a pool of light that stopped at a straight horizontal line and
   resumed at the next one. The cluster assignment numbered clusters
   slice-major; the shader read the grid texture row-major. Every fragment
   looked up a cluster belonging to some other part of the screen.

   Seven cluster tests passed. The ninth light *was* assigned, the runs *were*
   contiguous and ascending, and the tile-Y flip -- the axis that is genuinely
   easy to get backwards -- *was* right. Both sides were internally consistent
   and disagreed about a layout neither of them named.

   The general shape: **when two sides derive the same index independently, the
   index is the thing to write down, not the derivation.** `clusterIndexOf` is
   now that name, it is `constexpr`, and a test asserts it against the exact
   expression the shader computes. Recorded as D041.

2. **A gate that was already there found the first defect before a human could.**
   The environment prefilter was amortised -- one mip per frame, when the sky had
   moved -- which made a frame's SHAPE depend on its history. M4.5's
   `clock_differential` requires two frames differing only in `ClockTime` to
   issue the same NUMBER of commands, and it went red on the first run.

   The fix is a frame that uploads exactly one level ALWAYS, whether anything
   changed or not; only the baking is conditional. About thirty kilobytes
   averaged over the chain, for a property worth more than that. The claim that
   gate makes -- *what a clock changes is the values a frame carries, not its
   shape* -- turned out to be load-bearing for a feature written eight months
   after it.

3. **"It does nothing" was three different bugs wearing one symptom.** The
   anti-aliasing pass ran, and the staircase stayed.

   The reference FXAA uses the GREEN channel as luminance, on the argument that
   it carries most of the perceived signal. This engine's own test scene is a
   pink column on a blue floor: almost no green contrast, a great deal of
   luminance contrast, so every edge between them was skipped. That was the
   first bug, and Rec.601 luma fixed the detection.

   The staircase stayed. The second look was an INSTRUMENTED build -- output the
   local contrast, render, look at it -- which showed detection working perfectly
   along every edge and moved the search downstream. The third bug was in the
   blend: the subpixel term was squared once where Lottes shapes it with a
   smoothstep and then squares it.

   Both fixes are one line. Neither was findable by reading, and the middle step
   -- making the shader draw its own intermediate value -- is the one worth
   keeping. A shader has no other debugger.

4. **A golden was nearly re-recorded against a defect, which is the exact move M4
   made.** `screenshot_gate` went red during the instancing work and the reflex
   was to re-record it. It was not a pixel change at all: SDL was asserting on
   `instance_step_rate` and aborting the host mid-frame, so the gate was
   reporting a crash as a mismatch.

   What said so was cheap and should be the habit: the re-recorded file compared
   BYTE-IDENTICAL to the old one. **Before re-recording a golden, ask what
   changed -- and a byte-identical re-record is the cheapest possible answer to
   that question.**

5. **The instanced path was on, and it was doing nothing.** The horde scene
   reported 15,390 draw calls for 4,002 visible objects, which is what it
   reported before instancing existed.

   The sort key had learned about meshes but not about SECTIONS, and the enemy's
   glTF has two primitives sharing one material -- so its two halves interleaved
   by depth and every run was one draw long. `drawGeometryKey` packs both into
   the sixteen bits the key has for them.

   The general shape: **a feature that is switched on is not a feature that is
   working, and the difference is a number.** The roadmap knew this: it made the
   gate "draw calls beside visible objects" rather than "instancing implemented".

6. **0.18 is middle grey in units this engine does not have.** Automatic exposure
   maps the frame's average luminance onto a key, and 0.18 is photography's --
   which is middle grey in PHOTOMETRIC units. `Lighting.Brightness` defaults to
   2.6 because 2.6 looked right, not because it is 2.6 of anything.

   Borrowing the constant darkened every existing scene by about a stop and a
   third, and the honest fix was to name what the constant is: a CALIBRATION
   between an arbitrary radiance unit and display grey. It is 0.45, chosen so
   that scenes authored against the fixed exposure this pass replaced keep their
   level -- which makes automatic exposure a stabiliser rather than a change of
   level applied to all existing content.

7. **Automatic exposure removed the day/night cycle it was added to serve.** With
   no floor under the metered luminance, midnight opens the aperture until it
   matches noon -- and the day strip showed a night scene as bright as an
   afternoon one.

   A clamp fixes it, and the number in the clamp is the feature: against a key of
   0.45 the range allows a gain between 0.15 and 3, so night lands about a stop
   and a half below day. **A stabiliser with no limit is not a stabiliser, it is
   a flattener.**

8. **The day strip had been hiding an unanchored scene since M5.** Nothing in
   `tests/screenshots/daystrip` was `Anchored`, and an unanchored part has been a
   rigid body since M5. It never showed, because the strip renders one frame per
   host run and eight frames is not long enough to fall.

   It showed the moment each hour had to be HELD for forty frames so the exposure
   could settle: the scene dropped out of frame. **A harness that runs briefly
   cannot see a bug that takes time**, and it will keep not seeing it for as long
   as nothing asks it to run longer.

9. **ADR 0038 predicted four places the RHI freeze would break, and three of the
   predictions were wrong in the useful direction.** Cubemaps, mip-chain
   generation, compute for prefiltering, array textures for cascades -- each was
   answered by a different technique rather than a wider interface. Cascades live
   in an atlas addressed by `setViewport`; the environment is octahedral and its
   levels go up through the `uploadTexture` that already takes a level; the
   comparison sampler ADR 0038 asks for turned out to be `Gather` plus six ALU
   instructions.

   The one field that landed is the one a comment written BEFORE the freeze had
   already reserved by name. That is the freeze working exactly as ADR 0037
   intended: a backend need is a conversation, and the conversation mostly ends
   in "use the interface differently".

10. **A field documented "Reserved for future use. Must be set to 0" is a field
    where the plausible value is the wrong one.** `instance_step_rate = 1` for a
    per-instance stream reads as more correct than zero and aborts the process.
    It is also why the RHI's own field is a `bool` rather than a count: an
    interface offering a step rate would be offering something no backend here
    can honour.

11. **The frame cost the same at 320x180 and at 1920x1080, which is the M2
    horde's diagnosis arriving from the other direction.** `examples/02-meshes`
    measured 2.98 ms at 1080p against M4.5's 0.46, and the per-feature sweep
    accounted for less than a third of it. The resolution sweep is what named the
    rest: a cost identical at every resolution is not fragments.

    It was the CPU environment prefilter -- the feature this milestone had priced
    at zero. Three things were wrong and the same comparison found all three.
    `evaluateSky` called `pow` twice per evaluation and a full prefilter is a
    quarter of a million evaluations. The rebuild threshold was half a degree,
    which in a ninety-second day meant the chain rebuilt one level every frame
    forever and never got ahead of the sun.

    **And the job pool M7 built had no caller.** Nothing in the engine ever
    called `jobs::init`, so every `parallelFor` in it had been taking `jobs.h`'s
    documented serial path -- which the header calls a legitimate mode, and which
    is not the shipping one. M7 shipped a work-stealing pool with dependencies
    and a stable-commit discipline, and the first thing to notice it was not
    running was a frame-time measurement two milestones later.

    1.51 ms shipped, against 1.41 for the same scene with its sun frozen. **A
    feature nobody measured is a feature priced at zero**, and the general form
    is the one M2 already wrote down: when a cost does not move with resolution,
    stop looking at the GPU.

12. **A stage that is "shared" and pasted is two stages one edit apart.** M6's
    note in `pbr_skinned.hlsl` said the fragment stage "is shared and is
    `pbr.hlsl`'s, compiled again", and then said it in a second file with the
    whole function copied into it. That held while the function was one screen.
    This milestone gave it image-based lighting, cascades, a cluster lookup and
    an occlusion term -- and a third entry file for instancing, which would have
    been a third copy.

    The cost of extracting it was an afternoon and the saving was two shaders
    that cannot disagree. **The moment to extract a duplicate is when a third
    copy becomes possible, not when it appears.**

### What the reference is, and what it is not

The gate says **"'Looks better' is not a gate result — the comparison is against
a stated reference, or it is not a comparison"**, so here is the reference,
stated, in three parts and with the missing part named.

**Numerical, against closed forms.** The parts of image-based lighting that have
an exact answer are checked against that answer rather than against a picture:

- Irradiance from nine SH coefficients is compared against the cosine integral
  `(1/pi) * integral L(d) (n.d) dw`, computed in the test at 128x256 samples, for
  four normals, to 3%. **This is the pi**, and a factor of pi is "the scene looks
  a bit flat" — indistinguishable from a lighting preference until something
  asserts it.
- The GGX prefilter of a flat sky returns that flat value to 8e-3, which is the
  normalisation check: a weighted average of a constant is that constant.
- The split-sum BRDF table's ends are checked against the algebra: a mirror seen
  head-on has all its energy in the scale term and none in the Fresnel bias, and
  neither term may exceed one anywhere.
- The octahedral mapping round-trips to 1e-4 over a grid, and its landmarks —
  centre is up, corners are down — are asserted rather than assumed.

**Published parameters, against the papers they come from.** ADR 0038 scoped this
milestone as named techniques with published parameters precisely so this row
could exist: four cascades with GPU Gems 3's practical split scheme, Olsson and
Assarsson's clustering on Doom 2016's 16x9x24 grid with exponential slicing,
Karis's split sum, Jimenez's dual-filter bloom, Lottes's FXAA. Each is
implemented at the published parameters and each has its deviation written where
it deviates — the filter radius clamp, the atlas instead of an array, `Gather`
instead of a comparison sampler.

**Visually, against the previous milestone**, at the same camera and the same
clock. `docs/images/m7.5/` holds the pairs.

**What is NOT here, stated rather than skipped: no third-party renderer was
run.** The gate's phrase "a reference render" most naturally means one, and this
repository cannot produce one — no reference renderer is vendored, nothing in the
build can drive Unity or Unreal or the Khronos sample viewer, and a screenshot
taken by hand from an application nobody can re-run is not evidence a gate can
rest on. What replaces it is the three parts above, and the substitution is
recorded here rather than left for a reader to notice.

### What this milestone does not have

Stated here so a reviewer does not have to find it by looking.

- **No third-party reference render.** See above; it is the gate row this record
  substitutes for rather than satisfies.
- **The blended pass still cannot sample the depth it is attached to.** The depth
  prepass answers the roadmap's constraint for ambient occlusion and does not
  answer it for water foam, which needs a read-only depth attachment or a copy.
  One RHI field or one call, and an ADR either way.
- **No velocity buffer.** The jitter half of the temporal prerequisite ships as
  `RenderCamera::jitter` and is zero everywhere; the velocity half does not,
  because a second render target on every forward draw is renderer-wide bandwidth
  for a consumer that does not exist. The M7.5 brief's Decision 10 says exactly
  what it would take.
- **One environment for the whole world.** It is the sky, so it is right outdoors
  and wrong in a cave. Hand-placed reflection probes are the answer and v1 has no
  editor to place them with (ADR 0017).
- **Shadows are the sun's alone.** `RenderLight::shadows` is still the field
  nothing reads; a spot light casts none.
- **Alpha-masked geometry writes depth in the prepass and in the shadow pass**
  where its own fragments would have been discarded. The shadow pass has always
  had this gap; the prepass inherits it.
- **Skinned draws are never instanced**, because a joint palette is per draw.
  Transparent draws are never instanced either, and that one is correctness
  rather than a limitation: their order IS the result.
- **Graphics settings are not exposed.** Shadow resolution, cascade count,
  render scale, light budget and post toggles are all `constexpr`. ADR 0038 §3
  puts that family at M8 on purpose.
